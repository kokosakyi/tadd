CAN/CSA-015-15
Wood utility poles and reinforcing stubs
Plugged holes — holes that have been filled by forcing in a wood insert.
Red heart — a form of incipient decay, characterized by a reddish colour produced in the heartwood,
that does not render the wood unfit for the majority of uses.
Note: Firm red heart contains none of the white pockets that characterize the more advanced stage of decay. It is
not to be confused with naturally red heartwood.
Sap stain — a discoloration of the sapwood that is caused by the action of certain moulds and fungi and
is not accompanied by softening or other deterioration of the wood.
Scar (cat face) — a depression in the surface of a pole resulting from a wound where healing has not re-
established the normal cross-section of the pole.
Shake — separation along the grain, the greater part of which occurs between the annual growth rings.
Short crook — a localized deviation from straightness that, within any section 1.52 m (5 ft) or less in
length, is more than one-half the mean diameter of the crooked section (see Figure 2).
Spiral grain (twisted grain) — a type of growth in which the fibres take a spiral course around the trunk
of a tree instead of a vertical course.
Note: The spiral can extend around the trunk in a right-hand or left-hand direction or can alternate right and left
within the trunk. The degree of spirality in a pole is measured by the distance in feet along the axis of the pole in
which one complete twist of the spiral occurs.
Split — a separation along the grain forming an opening or fissure that extends through the piece from
one surface to another.
Sweep — the deviation of a pole from straightness (see Figure I).
