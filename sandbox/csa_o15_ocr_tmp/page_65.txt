It is recognized that a pole will be stub reinforced usually after it has served a substantial part of its
physical life. Consequently, a stub should have a life at least equal to the remaining life of the pole.
Experience has indicated that this result will generally be met by selecting a reinforcing stub whose
circumference at 1.83 m (6 ft) from the butt is approximately 51 mm (2 in) less than the circumference
at 1.83 m (6 ft) from the butt of a new replacement pole adequate to support the load. From these
selected bases, the dimensions of new stubs have been determined, the length and the groundline
circumference being designed to develop the strength of the longest pole of the highest class with
which the stub is intended to be associated. For example, a 3.05 m (IO ft) Class Z stub is designed to
develop the expected remaining strength of a 9.1 (30 ft) Class I pole.
