CAN/CSA-015-15
Wood utility poles and reinforcing stubs
Dead streak — a portion of the sapwood in which the life processes ceased prior to the cutting of the
tree.
Note: A dead streak differs from a wound such as a scar (cat face) in which the growth of new wood shows that
life processes are still acting to repair the injured part. It is characterized by a discoloured, weathered appearance
and a lack of growth of healthy wood overlapping the edges of the streak in an attempt to repair the damaged
area.
Decay (rot, dote, doze) — the deterioration of wood caused by the action of wood-destroying fungi,
resulting in softening, loss of strength and weight, and often change of texture and colour.
Advanced decay — the late stage of decay in which the decomposition is readily recognized as the
wood becomes punky, soft, stringy, pitted, or crumbly.
Incipient decay — the early stage of decay in which the decomposition has not proceeded far
enough to soften or otherwise change the hardness of the wood perceptibly. It is usually
accompanied by a slight discoloration or bleaching of the wood.
Note: Laboratory analysis may be required for positive identification of incipient decay.
Face of pole — the concave side of a pole having sweep in one plane or the side of greatest curvature
between the groundline and the top of a pole having a reverse or double sweep.
Groundline section — that section of a pole between 0.305 m (I ft) above and 0.61 m (2 ft) below the
groundline as specified in the pole dimension tables of this Standard.
Hollow heart — a void in the heartwood caused by decay or insect attack.
Insect damage — damage that is the result of boring in the wood by insects or insect larvae (limits as
defined in Clause 4-4.6).

Knot —

Dead knot — a knot resulting from a branch that died before the tree was cut.
Decayed knot (unsound knot) — a knot that is softer than the surrounding wood and shows
evidence of advanced decay.
Knot cluster — two or more knots grouped together as a unit, with the fibres of the wood being
deflected around the entire unit; a knot cluster is considered and measured as a single knot.
Ring knots — a group of knots that encircle a pole in the same plane; any knot intersecting an
imaginary line drawn around a pole is included in the measurement of the sum of diameters of ring
knots (see Table 1).
Spike knot — an elongated knot that results from a branch that grew on an angle not perpendicular
to the trunk of a tree.
Note: Spike knots are often encased in outer bark and/or tree sap, frequently resulting in a bark inclusion
(limits as defined in Clause 4.4.8.2).
Knot diameter — the dimension of a knot measured on the surface of a pole at a right angle to the
lengthwise axis of the pole.
Note: The sapwood as well as the heartwood portion ofa knot are included in the measurement.
Mechanical damage — surface damage incurred during the manufacturing or handling of poles.
