A.4 Coefficient of variation
If a coefficient of variation for any sample is desired, it may be calculated by expressing the appropriate
standard deviation as a percentage of the average value.
A.5 Sample exclusions
Some samples in Table A.I have been excluded from the weighted species averages in the rows labelled
"Average" because they were considered extraordinary on account of length, kerfing, treatment,
moisture content, or decay.
A.6 Moisture content
The moisture content of most specimens when tested was above the fibre saturation point, as required
by ASTM [)1036, which has been used since 1949 for the testing of utility poles. The fibre saturation
point is the moisture content level at which swelling of the wood ceases or shrinkage begins. A moisture
content above the fibre saturation point is a condition at the groundline recognized as the best
approximation for the purposes of this Standard.

A.8 Seasoning and treatment numbers
A.8.1
"Nil" in the "Treatment" column in Table A.I means no preservative treatment. Some of the samples
were tested in the green condition or in a condition of moisture content greater than the fibre
saturation point. Other samples were seasoned and then butt soaked before they were tested for
bending strength.
