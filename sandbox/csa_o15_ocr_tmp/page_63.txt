CAN/CSA-015-15
Annex B (informative)
Classification procedure for poles
Note: This Annex is not a mandatory part of this Standard.
B.I Derivation
B.I.I
Wood utility poles and reinforcing stubs
The minimum circumferences specified in Tables 4 to 13 have been calculated in order for each species
in a given class to develop groundline stresses approximately equal to those specified in Table 3 when a
given horizontal load is applied 0.61 m (2 ft) from the top of the pole. The horizontal loads used in the
calculations for separating the thirteen classes are specified in Table B.I.
B.I.2
In making the calculations, it was assumed that the pole is used as a simple cantilever and that the
maximum fibre stress in the pole subjected to the bending moment applied will occur at the groundline.
The circumference at the groundline was calculated using the standard engineering formula. This value
was then used to calculate the required circumference 1.83 m (6 ft) from the butt using a calculated
taper per foot of length between the groundline and 1.83 m (6 ft) from the butt. The calculated
circumference 1.83 m (6 ft) from the butt was rounded to the nearest 13 mm (1/2 in).
Note: A calculated taper is the difference between the minimum top circumference and the groundline
circumference divided by the length between them.
B.I.3
The assumption that the maximum fibre stress in the pole occurs at the groundline is theoretically
correct, provided that the circumference at the groundline is not more than 1-1/2 times the
circumference at the point of loading. If the circumference at the groundline is more than 1-1/2 times
the circumference at the point of loading, theoretically the maximum fibre stress in the pole occurs
above the groundline at the point where the circumference is 1-1/2 times that at the point of loading
(not necessarily the circumference 0.61 m (2 ft) from the top). When the load is not applied 0.61 m (2
ft) from the top, it is necessary for the class of pole to be determined by making separate calculations
from the specific loads to be supported.
Unless other information is available to substantiate the actual taper of the pole for a given species,
these calculations should be based on the taper determined by the difference in circumference
between the specified top dimension and the groundline dimension divided by the distance in feet
between the two measurements.
B.2 Shrinkage
The dimensions specified in Tables 4 to 13 are the minimum dimensions of the poles when offered to
the purchaser, regardless of the state of seasoning. While it is recognized that some shrinkage will occur
in seasoning, it is known that the dimensional loss is offset by an increase in fibre strength.
